# UK and international research

Most studies are still cross-sectional. Treat percentages as signals.

## UK universities

**University of Chichester** (Dr Benjamin Sharpe and colleagues):
- 313 UK student competitors (CS:GO, Valorant, R6) with Winchester and King’s College London. Stressors predicted poor sleep, burnout and social phobia; sleep strongly predicted anxiety, depression and distress (*Healthcare*, 2022). Sleep interventions are the first practical lever.
- Counter-Strike professionals: high rates of anxiety/depression symptoms in that sample (Birch et al., 2024).
- International mental health guidelines for esports (Movember; London convening with British Esports, GEF, Riot, BLAST, ESL FACEIT). Protocol: Poulus & Sharpe, *JEGE* 2025.
- BSc Psychology of Esports.

**Nottingham / NTU:** women and TGD competitive gamers — very high gender-related harassment, avoidance coping, poorer mental health.

**Manchester #BeeWell** (25,000 pupils 11–14): no evidence that hours on games *alone* caused next-year rises in anxiety or depression. Content and support matter more than raw time.

**Birkbeck (Pontes et al.):** professional gamers had higher gaming-disorder prevalence than matched non-professionals (~3.3% vs 1.7%) — still a minority.

## Reviews

Poulus et al. scoping review (2026, Movember): 89 studies; anxiety 38–82%, depression 25–37%; few trials. GG Project (Erasmus+/G2 partners): high sleep disturbance in 1,000+ players.

Take-home: fix sleep first; student leagues are a reachable UK population; elite orgs have more psych support than ranked players.
